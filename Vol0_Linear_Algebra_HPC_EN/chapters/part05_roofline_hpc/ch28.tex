\section{Communication and Scaling}
A communication phase may be approximated by
\[
 T_{\rm comm}\approx n_{\rm msg}\alpha+V/B_{\rm net},
\]
where $\alpha$ is effective message-startup latency, $V$ is the explicitly defined byte volume, and $B_{\rm net}$ is effective network bandwidth, all sensitive to protocol, topology, congestion, and concurrency. Nonoverlapped costs sum, whereas ideal overlap yields the lower bound
\[
 T\gtrsim\max(F/P_{\rm peak},Q/B_{\rm mem},T_{\rm comm}).
\]
Realized runtime is further governed by communication progress, contention, and dependency chains.
Under strong scaling at fixed global size, shrinking a cubic subdomain of side $\ell$ scales volume work as $O(\ell^3)$ and face exchange as $O(\ell^2)$, steadily raising the surface-to-volume ratio. Under weak scaling at fixed local work, efficiency remains constrained by global reductions, network contention, and coarse-grid solves.
\section{Halos, Reductions, and Overlap}
Regular stencils exchange neighboring grid faces, FVM and DG exchange face states or traces (with higher order increasing both face data and local arithmetic), and Yee exchanges follow the two-phase magnetic--electric dependence. Krylov solvers add dot-product allreduces to operator halos: short CG recurrences still require global synchronization, and GMRES orthogonalization can increase reduction counts.
Nonblocking patterns post receives and sends first, compute halo-independent interiors, and then wait before updating boundaries; actual MPI progress and overlap depend on the library and runtime configuration and are verified by timing.\cite{mpi}
Pipelined Krylov methods overlap reductions with computation, while communication-avoiding block or s-step variants reduce synchronization frequency at the risk of basis ill-conditioning, extra work, and residual drift, requiring concurrent numerical-stability checks.

\section{Actual Directions in FGMRES}
At step $j$ of right-preconditioned FGMRES, computing $\bm z_j=M_j^{-1}\bm v_j$ and orthogonalizing $A\bm z_j$ yields
\[
 AZ_m=V_{m+1}\bar H_m,\qquad
 \bm x_m=\bm x_0+Z_m\bm y_m.
\]
When the preconditioner is a fixed linear operator, $Z_m$ represents the basis under that single inverse action. Variable-tolerance inner solves, adaptive AMG cycles, or nonlinear smoothers do not share a fixed map, so FGMRES stores the actual correction vectors $\bm z_j$ to preserve an exact projection relation.\cite{petsc}
\conceptbridge{Why use a flexible outer method if the inner problems are nearly identical?}{An inner solve governed by a residual tolerance can behave as a slightly different linear map on every call. An outer solver built for a fixed preconditioner then suffers a mismatch between its orthogonal basis and its solution update, whereas a flexible method stores and combines the exact correction directions produced.}{Changing inner action $\longrightarrow$ actual correction directions $\longrightarrow$ flexible projection.}
\section{Combinations and Minimal Examples}
Comparing MINRES and GMRES on a symmetric indefinite two-by-two system highlights the symmetry savings of short recurrences, while alternating one and two relaxation sweeps on Poisson illustrates the first two columns of $Z_m$. Block Krylov methods share subspace information across multiple right-hand sides, and subspace recycling accelerates sequences of related time-step or parameter solves at the cost of extra basis storage and updates.
